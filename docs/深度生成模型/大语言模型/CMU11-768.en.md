# CMU 11-768: AI Agents

## Descriptions

- Offered by: Carnegie Mellon University
- Prerequisites: Prior experience training neural language models. Background equivalent to CMU 11-667, 11-711, or 10-202 is recommended.
- Programming Languages: Python
- Difficulty: 🌟🌟🌟🌟
- Class Hour: 100 hours

Taught by Graham Neubig and Daniel Fried, this course explores LLM-based agents that use tools, context management, memory, and planning to complete multi-step tasks. Topics include coding agents, computer-use agents, supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, agent frameworks, safety, human-agent interaction, and multi-agent interaction.

The course emphasizes hands-on implementation through three individual assignments covering agent harnesses, evaluation, and training, followed by a research project in the second half of the semester. It is intended for learners with experience training language models who want to study how to build, evaluate, and train agents. The difficulty rating and workload are estimates for independent study. The 100-hour estimate includes lectures, readings, and the three assignments, but excludes the full research project.

As of October 2026, the Fall 2026 course is ongoing, and recordings, slides, and assignment materials are still being released. Some recordings and assignment materials are publicly available, while submissions use Canvas for CMU students only. Some practical work requires model APIs and cloud services; independent learners should check resource requirements and costs.
